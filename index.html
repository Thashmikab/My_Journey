The question

Every wind turbine is also a materials question.

Renewables cut emissions at the point of generation. But each turbine, solar farm and cable is built from metals that are mined, refined and shipped first. My research follows those materials through their whole life and asks where the environmental burden really ends up.

Dynamic life cycle assessment

Material impacts are tracked year by year as the grid and supply chains change, rather than as one static number.

Net impact, not gross

Each material's burden is weighed against the fossil generation it helps displace, to show whether it helps or harms the environment overall.

Multi-objective optimisation

NSGA-II searches UK deployment roadmaps to 2050 that balance environmental impacts against material use.

The decision

Hundreds of good answers. Which one do you build?

An optimiser doesn't return one best roadmap. It returns a Pareto front, a whole set of solutions where improving one objective means giving up some of another.

The knee point is where that trade-off turns sharp. Past it, a small gain in one objective starts costing a lot in the others. I built paretolens to find it, rank the rest and explain the choice to people who don't read code.
